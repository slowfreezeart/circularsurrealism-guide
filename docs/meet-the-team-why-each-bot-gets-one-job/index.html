Meet the team: five Grok Bots, one job each, and how the work flows

Meet the five Grok Bots behind the guides and the free hub: Consu, Scribe, Herald, Lokus and Finn. One job each, one shared computer, and how work reaches Slowfreeze.

Behind every guide, article and post in this series is a small team of Grok Bots. There are five of them, each with one job. Slowfreeze, an AI artist who is not a developer, makes every final call. This article is the introduction: who each bot is, when each one joined, and why the split works.

Can bots really work as a team?

Yes. On Day 0, 25 October 2026, that was the first question Slowfreeze asked. Consu's answer: each bot has its own chat, memory and personality, so you can give each one a clear role. They share one computer, so files, tools and logins that one bot sets up are there for the others. What they do not share is a screen, so two bots can browse at the same time without getting in each other's way. (Source: Consu chat, Oct 25; timeline.)

That answer became the design of the team. Shared computer means shared files. Separate chats and memories mean separate jobs.

Who is on the team, and when did each bot join?

  • Consu, Team Lead. Day 0, 25 October. The first bot, and the one that coordinates everyone else. Consu was formally set as "Team Lead" on 26 October. (Timeline, Oct 25 and Oct 26.)
  • Scribe, articles and hub. Created 25 October. Scribe writes the free articles and builds the hub at guide.circularsurrealism.com. The hub's first commit was 26 October at 12:27 AM CT. (Timeline, Oct 25 and Oct 26; outline item 3.)
  • Herald, social and community. Created 25 October. Herald runs the brand X account, community replies and outreach. (Timeline, Oct 25.)
  • Lokus, visuals and covers. Joined 25 October as "Image wizard" and made the three guide covers. The name Lokus first shows up on 27 October. Lokus now makes the covers for the articles. (Timeline, Oct 25, Oct 27 and Nov 13.)
  • Finn, Animator. Created 27 October for skits and short films. (Timeline, Oct 27.)

The label on Scribe's profile was tidied to "Scribe • Writer" on 9 November. (Timeline, Nov 9.)

What does each bot actually do?

Consu coordinates. Consu plans the work, hands tasks to the other bots, sets up the Gumroad store, collects approvals and sends reports. On Day 0 Consu built the first guide blueprint, and the three Gumroad products went live at 11:38 PM CT the same night. (Timeline, Oct 25.)

Scribe writes. Articles, search titles and descriptions, and the hub. Every article is checked against real sources before it is drafted, which is why this series has a facts timeline behind it.

Herald talks to people. Social posts, replies in the community and the brand account. Herald's job is to start conversations, not to publish anything on its own.

Lokus designs. Covers, charts and visuals, in black with dark gold and teal captions. One cover per article.

Finn animates. Skits and short films starring Slowfreeze's AI Art. By 2 November, 21 skits had been made, 20 of them with post drafts. Skits use only characters from the Based Knights collection. Short films run 15 seconds to 1 minute 30 seconds and are made with Seedance 2.5 and Seedance 2.0. (Timeline, Oct 29 and Nov 2.)

Why does each bot get one job?

The team's rule is simple: give each bot one lane, and no bot edits another bot's work. Three reasons it works:

  1. Clear ownership. When a cover looks wrong, you know it is a Lokus question. When a number in an article is off, it is a Scribe question. Nobody guesses.
  2. Smaller memory, better focus. Each bot only has to remember its own rules. Scribe remembers the wording rules. Finn remembers which Knights have already been used. Neither carries the other's checklist.
  3. Fewer collisions. Because all five share one computer, a bot that edits another bot's files can quietly undo its work. One lane each keeps files where they belong.

The same idea shows up in a rule Finn follows: on 29 October, Slowfreeze set a standing rule that skits use only Based Knights characters. One rule, one owner. (Timeline, Oct 29.)

How does work flow from idea to Slowfreeze?

The path is short, and it always runs through Consu:

  1. Slowfreeze gives Consu a request. In chat, or on a call.
  2. Consu splits it. Writing goes to Scribe, social to Herald, visuals to Lokus, animation to Finn. Each gets a clear brief and a file path to save to.
  3. Each bot works in its own lane and reports back to Consu with the file paths.
  4. Consu checks and bundles the results, then brings them to Slowfreeze.
  5. Slowfreeze approves, edits or says no. Nothing goes out until that last step. The next article in the series covers that approval flow in full.

What has the team produced so far?

A few honest, specific numbers from the first 19 days, all from the timeline:

  • Guides: Quick Start at $19 and The House Guide at $39, which went live on Day 0 and now run to 21 modules and 114 lessons.
  • Hub: 9 free articles live at guide.circularsurrealism.com.
  • X Articles: 15 logged on @Slowfreeze98 between 26 October and 13 November, averaging about 200 to 350 views each by Slowfreeze's own estimate.
  • Skits: 21 made. They are posted on their own, with no quote-repost boost. On average they get about 150 views on X and about 250 on TikTok. The best ones reached 600 to 700 views on Instagram. These are Slowfreeze's own estimates from 13 November, and the best case is not typical.
  • First free download: on 12 November, someone downloaded the free Performance Checklist after finding it through the hub.

These are early numbers from a small team. They are real, and they are the baseline the rest of the series builds on.

Can you build your own team?

Yes. The shape is easy to copy: start with one bot that coordinates, give every new bot one clear job, and keep your approval as the last step. Slowfreeze published the Team Lead setup as a template for others to use (version 5, staged 29 October). (Timeline, Oct 29; Slowfreeze via Consu, Nov 13.) No coding experience necessary. You need a clear job description for each bot.
