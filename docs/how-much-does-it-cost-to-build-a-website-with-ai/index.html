How much does it cost to build a website with AI? The real bill behind a 400+ prompt artist site

How much does it cost to build a website with AI? About $100 with a subscription, about $400 to $500 without, versus Grok Build's estimate to hire it out.

Most answers to "how much does it cost to build a website with AI" are either a pricing page or a guess. This one is a real bill, from one real site.

circularsurrealism.com is an AI artist's museum-style site: 440+ works in 27 collections, with live sales data, a print shop, wallet connect, deep zoom and themes. Slowfreeze vibe coded it in Grok Build, in one long conversation: 400+ prompts from 27 August to 23 October 2026. Here is what it cost, what Grok Build estimated it would cost to hire out, and what those numbers do and don't mean.

How much did this AI-built website actually cost?

  • With a subscription: about $100. That is what was actually paid: the $11-a-month X plan the artist was already paying for at the time, plus at least $75 in credit packs.
  • Without a subscription: about $400 to $500. That is the artist's own estimate of what the same build would cost if you didn't already have the plan.

So the about $100 only works because the plan was already there. Without it, the honest number is about $400 to $500. Plans and prices change, so check the current ones before you plan around these.

How much would it cost to hire someone to build it?

On 16 October, near the end of the build, the artist asked Grok Build what the site would cost if someone else had built it. These are Grok Build's estimates, not quotes. Nobody bid on this site.

Who builds it Grok Build's estimate
A studio About $90,000 to $160,000
A developer using AI agents, while you only approve About $8,000 to $20,000
You, with Grok, the way this one was made About $100 with a subscription, about $400 to $500 without (the artist's own figures)

Grok Build's words on the studio figure: "That is the cost of the work, not your Grok bill."

Grok Build also gave a general figure: a build this long "usually lands around $150 to $400 in tool money". That is Grok Build's typical figure, not this build's. This build's own numbers are the ones above.

Why is the estimate so much higher than the bill?

Because the estimate prices the work, not the tools. Grok Build counted about 45,000 lines in the site: 18,900 of them the program itself, and 26,000 the catalog, articles and process text. Then it named the parts that keep the number high:

  • three-chain wallet connect, on the phone and on desktop;
  • live auction marks;
  • the visit counter;
  • deep zoom;
  • the light video cards;
  • nine atmospheres.

And its reasoning for why hiring a developer with AI agents still costs thousands: you are paying for someone's weeks of steering, checking the phone and fixing taste. In its words:

"An agent types the code. Someone still has to know what the museum is supposed to be."

How much does an artist website cost, really?

It depends on what you count. These numbers leave out a few things, on purpose:

  • An estimate is not a quote. A real studio or developer could quote higher or lower.
  • Your time isn't counted. The about $100 is what left the wallet. It doesn't price 400+ prompts over about 8 weeks, checking every room on a phone, saying "no, not like that" until it was right.
  • The material was ready. Years of AI Art, already on the chains and marketplaces, with the artist's own words for it. The builder arranged it; it didn't invent it.
  • The subscription did a lot of the work. Without it, the artist's own estimate is about $400 to $500.

So the takeaway isn't "a $100 website". It's that the cost moved: from paying someone for the work, to doing the deciding yourself, with the AI doing the typing.

How do you keep the cost of building with AI down?

The build that produced these numbers also produced the lessons for spending less on the next one:

  • Keep one site in one conversation, and write prompts that are specific, visual and final. See how to build a website with AI, step by step.
  • Don't pay for a rebuild you don't need. When the preview was lost, asking for a rebuild cost credits. Restoring from the live site was the fix. See how to recover a lost Grok Build preview.
  • Back up before you need to. A backup turns the next loss into a non-event.

Should you hire, or build it yourself?

A studio is sometimes worth it. If your time is worth more than the gap, or you don't want to sit with a preview for weeks, hiring is a real option.

But if you have the material and the patience, and you know what your site is supposed to be, you can get very far yourself. The case-study site was built by an AI artist, not a developer, by describing what he wanted, looking at the preview, and saying what was wrong.
